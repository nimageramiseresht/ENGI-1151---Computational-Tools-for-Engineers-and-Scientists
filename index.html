1Introduction

Monte Carlo simulation is a technique that uses randomness to solve problems that might be deterministic in principle. It is widely used in engineering to model uncertainty and simulate complex systems.

The method is named after the Monte Carlo Casino in Monaco, because of its reliance on chance. Instead of solving problems with exact formulas, Monte Carlo methods use repeated random sampling to approximate the answer.

Example 1. Two cars travel at speeds that are uniformly distributed between 65 km/h and 132 km/h. If they travel in opposite directions, what is their relative speed, represented as a probability distribution?

Some basic arithmetic operations can be carried out on probability distributions directly. When the behaviour of a system becomes more complex, however, it is very hard to determine analytically the distribution that describes it. Consider the next example.

Example 2. The average travel speed on a two-lane highway depends on the traffic composition, specifically the number of cars and trucks on the road. In a simple representation, the average travel speed V (km/h) can be estimated from the total volume and the truck proportion:

$$V = V_f\left(1 - \alpha\,\frac{N_c + N_t}{C} - \beta\,p_t\right)$$

where:

  • \(V_f\) is the free-flow speed (100 km/h)
  • \(C\) is the capacity of the road (3000 veh/h)
  • \(\alpha\) is the congestion sensitivity coefficient, Uniform[0.6, 0.9]
  • \(\beta\) is the truck impact factor, Uniform[0.3, 0.6]
  • \(N_c\) is the number of cars per hour, Normal(\(\mu = 1200,\ \sigma = 486\))
  • \(N_t\) is the number of trucks per hour, Normal(\(\mu = 329,\ \sigma = 83\))
  • \(p_t\) is the ratio of trucks to total vehicles

Monte Carlo simulation was developed in the 1940s by scientists working on the Manhattan Project. Stanislaw Ulam and John von Neumann first used it to study neutron diffusion. It has since become a powerful tool in many scientific and engineering disciplines.

2How Monte Carlo simulation works

  1. Define the problem. Identify the output of interest and the uncertain input parameters.
  2. Assign probability distributions. Describe the uncertainty of each input with an appropriate distribution.
  3. Random sampling. Generate many random input samples from these distributions.
  4. Determine the outputs. Calculate the system output for each random sample. This can be a Python function, a simple equation, or a large, complex simulation model.
  5. Analyse the results. Compute summary statistics such as the mean, standard deviation and confidence intervals of the outputs.

3Random numbers

Programming languages usually generate pseudorandom numbers: numbers that appear random but come from deterministic algorithms. They are not truly random, but they are good enough for most simulations, so from here on we simply call them random numbers.

Python can generate random numbers from many well-known distributions, including uniform, normal, beta, chi-square and binomial.

Differs from the PDF. The handout writes np.random.rand((2, 3)). rand takes each dimension as a separate argument, so the tuple version raises a TypeError. Use np.random.rand(2, 3), or np.random.random((2, 3)) if you prefer a tuple.

We can also draw from other distributions.

Random numbers from the uniform distribution on [0, 1] are usually all you need for any probabilistic analysis. For example, a sample from an arbitrary uniform distribution \(U(a, b)\) is

$$U(a, b) = a + U(0, 1)\,(b - a)$$

Differs from the PDF. The handout gives \((U(0,1) + a)(b - a)\). That expression spans \([a(b-a),\ (1+a)(b-a)]\), not \([a, b]\): for \(a = 0.6,\ b = 0.9\) it gives values between 0.18 and 0.48. Scale first, then shift, as above. Run the check below to see the difference.

5.2 Fitting a normal distribution

Another way to assess the results is to fit a normal distribution to them and see how closely the histogram follows it. The normal distribution is not just “a distribution”: it describes the behaviour of many natural phenomena, so it is worth testing results against it.

The probability density function of the normal distribution with mean \(\mu\) and standard deviation \(\sigma\) is

$$f(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^2}}\exp\!\left(-\frac{(x-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}\right)$$

6Applications in engineering

Monte Carlo simulation is used in many engineering applications, including structural reliability analysis, circuit noise modelling, project cost estimation and risk analysis in manufacturing.
